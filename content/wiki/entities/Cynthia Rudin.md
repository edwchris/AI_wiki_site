# Cynthia Rudin

Professor of Computer Science, Electrical and Computer Engineering, Statistics, and Mathematics at Duke University. One of the foremost proponents of interpretable machine learning and a critic of post-hoc explanation ("explainable AI") approaches.

## Key contribution to this wiki

**[[Stop Explaining Black Box ML — Rudin 2019]]** — argues that for high-stakes decisions, inherently interpretable models should be used instead of opaque models with post-hoc explanations. Core arguments:
- The accuracy–interpretability trade-off is largely a myth (Rashomon set argument)
- Post-hoc explanations are unfaithful approximations
- Black boxes create undetectable errors and cannot integrate contextual information

Winner of multiple awards including the INFORMS Franz Edelman Award. Named one of the "most powerful women in AI" by Forbes.

## CORELS model

Rudin's lab developed CORELS (Certifiably Optimal RulE ListS) — a 3-rule decision list that matches the accuracy of COMPAS (130+ factors) for recidivism prediction. Used as the central example in the 2019 paper.
