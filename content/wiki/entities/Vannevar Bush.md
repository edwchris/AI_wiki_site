# Vannevar Bush

**Kind:** Person — Engineer, science administrator, visionary

## Overview
American engineer and science administrator (1890–1974). Best known in information science for his 1945 essay "As We May Think" (The Atlantic), which proposed the **[[Memex]]** — a personal, mechanized knowledge store with associative trails between documents. Considered a foundational text in hypertext theory and personal information management.

## Key contributions
- **"As We May Think"** (1945, The Atlantic) — essay describing the Memex concept
- **[[Memex]]** — hypothetical device for personal knowledge storage with associative indexing (precursor to hypertext and the web)
- Directed the US Office of Scientific Research and Development in WWII
- Helped establish the National Science Foundation

## [[Memex]] concept
The [[Memex]] was envisioned as a desk with microfilm storage, allowing users to create "trails" — associative links between documents. Unlike the web (which went hierarchical/search-based), Bush's vision was **private and actively curated**, with the connections as valuable as the documents. The unsolved problem was who would maintain the trails and keep them current.

> [!note] Connection to [[LLM Wiki Pattern]]
> [[Andrej Karpathy]] explicitly cites Bush as the spiritual predecessor to the LLM Wiki Pattern. The LLM solves the maintenance problem Bush couldn't: who keeps the associative links current as knowledge grows.

## Relationship to [[LLM Wiki Pattern]]
- The wiki pattern is the realized form of the Memex vision
- LLMs replace the human archivist Bush assumed would be needed
- The "associative trails" Bush described map to the wikilink graph in Obsidian
