# Raja Parasuraman

American cognitive neuroscientist and human factors researcher (1950–2015). Professor at George Mason University. One of the most influential researchers in the psychology of automation, attention, and human error. Founder of the field of neuroergonomics.

## Key contributions to this wiki

**[[Humans and Automation — Parasuraman and Riley 1997]]** — the foundational taxonomy of human–automation interaction:
- Stage × level framework (4 stages × 10 levels)
- Four outcome patterns: use, misuse (automation bias), disuse (algorithm aversion), abuse

**[[A Model for Types and Levels of Human Interaction with Automation — Parasuraman et al. 2000]]** — expanded taxonomy; 10-level automation scale across 4 cognitive function types; formal cost-benefit framework for automation level selection

**[[Complacency and Bias in Human Use of Automation — Parasuraman and Manzey 2010]]** — distinguishes complacency (monitoring failure) from automation bias (decision failure); attentional resource account of both

## Significance

Parasuraman's stage × level framework is the standard vocabulary for discussing human-AI interaction levels. Any design decision about how much authority to give an AI system — whether in aviation, nuclear power, or clinical medicine — can be expressed in his terms.
