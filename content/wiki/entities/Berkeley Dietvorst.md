# Berkeley Dietvorst

Behavioral scientist and marketing professor. At time of the algorithm aversion paper: doctoral student at the Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania (co-authors: Joseph Simmons and Cade Massey). Subsequently faculty at University of Chicago Booth School of Business.

## Key contribution to this wiki

**[[Algorithm Aversion — Dietvorst et al. 2015]]** — coined the term *algorithm aversion*: the finding that people abandon algorithms after seeing them err even once, even when the algorithm still outperforms human alternatives. Five experiments using forecasting tasks.

Key follow-on finding: when participants could modify the algorithm's output (even minimally), algorithm aversion reversed — they used the algorithm significantly more. This "algorithm appreciation" via agency is directly applicable to clinical AI interface design.

## Significance

Dietvorst's work reframes the AI adoption problem. The clinical AI literature overwhelmingly focuses on automation bias (over-reliance). Algorithm aversion shows the opposite failure mode is equally real and produces different design prescriptions. Both failures stem from miscalibrated trust — Dietvorst's contribution is demonstrating the under-trust case empirically.
