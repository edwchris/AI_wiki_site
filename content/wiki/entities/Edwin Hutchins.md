# Edwin Hutchins

American cognitive anthropologist. Developed distributed cognition theory through ethnographic studies of real-world cognitive systems — most famously the navigation systems aboard US Navy ships.

## Key contribution to this wiki

[[How a Cockpit Remembers Its Speeds — Hutchins 1995]] — demonstrates that a cockpit's memory for approach speeds is stored not in pilots' heads but in the sociotechnical system of speed cards, gauges, and verbal protocols. When one element of this system fails, the whole cognitive system may fail.

Also foundational for [[Distributed Cognition — Hollan et al. 2000]].

## Core idea

Cognition is not just inside heads — it is distributed across people, artifacts, and time. The unit of cognitive analysis should be the system, not the individual. This reframes AI as a cognitive artifact that becomes part of a distributed clinical cognitive system.

## Relevance to clinical AI

When AI is introduced into a clinical workflow, it doesn't just assist the clinician — it restructures the entire distributed cognitive system. The cognitive properties of the AI (what it knows, when it knows it, how it communicates) reshape how the human and the technology jointly accomplish tasks. Understanding this requires Hutchins-style system-level analysis.
