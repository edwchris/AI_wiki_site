# James D. Lee

Human factors researcher, Professor at the University of Wisconsin-Madison and later Cornell University. Primary contributor to the theoretical framework of trust in automation, including calibration theory and design principles for appropriate reliance.

## Key contribution to this wiki

**[[Trust in Automation — Lee and See 2004]]** — the definitive trust calibration framework:
- Trust defined as "the attitude that an agent will help achieve an individual's goals in a situation characterized by uncertainty and vulnerability"
- Three bases of trust: performance (reliability), process (transparency), purpose (intent alignment)
- Design principles for calibrating trust to actual reliability

## Core insight

Neither high trust nor low trust is the goal — *calibrated* trust is the goal. Miscalibration in either direction degrades human–automation performance. This is the lens through which both [[Automation Bias]] (over-trust) and [[Algorithm Aversion]] (under-trust) should be understood.
