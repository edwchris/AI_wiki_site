Japanese organizational theorist; Professor Emeritus at the Graduate School of International Corporate Strategy, Hitotsubashi University, Tokyo. Pioneer of organizational knowledge creation theory; often called the "father of knowledge management."

## Core Contribution
- [[Dynamic Theory of Organizational Knowledge Creation — Nonaka 1994]] — foundational paper introducing the SECI model (Socialization, Externalization, Combination, Internalization) of knowledge conversion
- *The Knowledge-Creating Company* (1995, with Hirotaka Takeuchi) — extended the SECI model into a theory of competitive advantage through organizational knowledge creation

## Key Concepts
- [[Tacit and Explicit Knowledge]]: the distinction between embody-able know-how and transmissible know-that
- The SECI spiral: knowledge creation as a continuous upward spiral from individual to organizational level
- Ba: the shared context or "place" in which knowledge creation occurs

## Relevance to AI
Nonaka's framework provides the theoretical grounding for understanding what AI can and cannot capture. AI training is primarily a Combination-mode process over explicit data; it cannot participate in Socialization or Internalization as humans do. Training labels represent a lossy Externalization of tacit expert judgment — the core of the [[AI Ground Truth]] problem.
