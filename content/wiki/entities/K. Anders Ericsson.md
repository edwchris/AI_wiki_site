# K. Anders Ericsson

Swedish-American cognitive psychologist (1947–2020). Conradi Eminent Scholar and Professor of Psychology at Florida State University. Originator of deliberate practice theory — perhaps the most influential psychological framework for understanding expert performance.

## Key contribution to this wiki

**[[Deliberate Practice in Medicine — Ericsson 2004]]** — applies the deliberate practice framework specifically to medicine. Key findings:
- Expert performance requires defined task goals, immediate feedback, and repeated opportunities — not just accumulated experience
- Peer-nominated "expert" physicians do not consistently outperform average physicians in diagnostic accuracy
- Experience alone leads to performance plateau; deliberate practice maintains and raises performance
- Older expert musicians maintain performance through weekly practice — expertise is not permanent without continued effort

## Core theoretical contribution

The *10-year rule* and *10,000 hours* framing (from chess and music research) popularised by Malcolm Gladwell in *Outliers* originated with Ericsson's empirical work. Ericsson's own emphasis: hours of *deliberate* practice matter; hours of naive practice do not.

## Relevance to AI and deskilling

Ericsson provides the psychological mechanism for deskilling in AI contexts: when automation removes the challenge that generates deliberate practice opportunities, the development and maintenance of expert skill is arrested. This is the scientific basis for the concern that AI in medical training may produce graduates who perform well with AI but cannot operate without it.
