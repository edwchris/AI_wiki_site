# SERVQUAL

A research instrument for measuring customer perceptions of service quality by comparing expectations to actual experience. Developed by **A. Parasuraman**, Valarie Zeithaml, and Leonard Berry (1988).

> [!note] A. Parasuraman (marketing researcher, University of Miami) is a different person from [[Raja Parasuraman]] (human factors researcher, George Mason University). Same surname, entirely different fields.

---

## Five Dimensions (RATER)

| Dimension | Definition |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Reliability** | Ability to perform the promised service accurately and dependably |
| **Assurance** | Knowledge, competence, and courtesy of staff; ability to inspire trust |
| **Tangibles** | Physical facilities, equipment, and appearance of personnel |
| **Empathy** | Caring, individualised attention to customers |
| **Responsiveness** | Willingness to help and provide prompt service |

---

## The Gaps Model

Service quality failures arise from five gaps between provider and customer:

| Gap | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| **Gap 1** (Knowledge) | Management misunderstands what customers expect |
| **Gap 2** (Standards) | Management fails to translate understanding into service specifications |
| **Gap 3** (Delivery) | Staff fail to deliver to specification (training, technology, role conflict) |
| **Gap 4** (Communications) | External communications overpromise what can be delivered |
| **Gap 5** (Service quality) | Difference between expected and perceived service — the only directly measurable gap |

Gap 5 is what SERVQUAL measures. Gaps 1–4 have diagnostic value for explaining where failures originate.

---

## Healthcare applications

SERVQUAL has been adapted for healthcare (sometimes called HEALTHQUAL):
- Measures patient perception of care quality across RATER dimensions
- Particularly used in outpatient and primary care settings
- Has been applied to radiology and imaging services to assess patient satisfaction

## Relevance to this wiki

- Service quality framing complements [[Human-Centered AI Evaluation]] — AI implementation changes the delivery gap (Gap 3) and the communications gap (Gap 4)
- AI systems that improve accuracy but reduce empathy and responsiveness may worsen SERVQUAL scores even while improving clinical outcomes
- Sonographer-patient relationship studies (e.g., [[Caring Relationship Between Sonographer and Patient — Van Der Westhuizen et al. 2020]]) map onto the Empathy dimension

---

## Limitations

- Expectation concept is theoretically ambiguous (aspirational vs. predicted)
- Five-dimension structure is not universally replicable across all service contexts
- Generic instrument requires modification for specific healthcare domains
