Framework for expert skill acquisition developed by K. Anders Ericsson; argues that exceptional performance emerges from structured, effortful practice under specific conditions — not mere accumulated experience.

## Core Conditions (Ericsson)
1. **Well-defined task** with a clear performance standard
2. **Immediate, informative feedback** so errors can be detected and corrected
3. **Opportunities for repetition** with variation to challenge and extend skills
4. **Expert guidance or supervision** to direct attention to the right features
5. **High cognitive effort** — deliberate practice is effortful and not enjoyable in the way flow states are

## Routine Practice vs. Deliberate Practice
| | Routine Practice | Deliberate Practice |
|-|-----------------|---------------------|
| Effect on skill | Maintains current level | Raises current level |
| Cognitive load | Low (automated responses) | High (conscious attention) |
| Goal | Efficiency, throughput | Improvement |
| Transfer | Limited | Broad |

## Application to Sonography
[[Deliberate Practice in Sonography — Edwards et al. 2018]] argues that sonography curricula must embed DP principles:
- Technical scanning skills (probe handling, angle, pressure) develop through DP, not passive clinical exposure
- Expert sonographers have extensive [[Tacit and Explicit Knowledge]] built through structured skill-building cycles
- Clinical hour accumulation without DP conditions produces experienced-but-not-expert practitioners

## AI Implications
- AI tools that automate scanning mechanics (plane detection, measurement → [[PROMETHEUS RCT — Day et al. 2025]]) reduce the number of effortful deliberate-practice repetitions available to trainees
- Risk: students may achieve clinical throughput goals without developing the underlying tacit competence
- Counter-argument: AI could free cognitive bandwidth for higher-level deliberate practice on clinical reasoning
- Long-term deskilling risk if AI is embedded before foundational competence is established
- [[AI-Enhanced Cardiac US Training — Karni et al. 2025]]: RCT showing AI guidance improves *short-term* novice performance on apical cardiac views — but does not test retention or transfer without AI. The key unresolved question: does AI-guided acquisition build genuine DP-style competence, or scaffold performance without building the underlying skill?

## Broader Relevance
- Deliberate practice is structurally a process of **Internalization** in Nonaka's terms (converting explicit feedback into tacit competence) → [[Tacit and Explicit Knowledge]]
- AI cannot perform Internalization in the same embodied sense — it does not learn-by-doing across sessions
