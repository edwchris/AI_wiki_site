# MAIRS-MS

**Medical Artificial Intelligence Readiness Scale for Medical Students** — a validated survey instrument measuring AI preparedness across four domains in health professions students and graduates.

Deployed in [[Measuring AI Preparedness in Health Professions Education — Edwards et al. 2026]] (Radiography, 2026) to survey Australian medical radiation science (MRS) students and graduates.

---

## The Four Domains

| Domain | What it measures |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Cognition** | Understanding of AI — how it works, its limitations, when outputs can be trusted |
| **Ability** | Practical capability to use AI tools in clinical workflows |
| **Vision** | Perception of AI's current and future role in the profession |
| **Ethics** | Awareness of ethical dimensions: bias, accountability, patient consent, data governance |

---

## Key Finding from Edwards et al. 2026

Across Australian MRS students/graduates:
- **Ethics** was the highest-scoring domain — respondents recognised ethical issues even without technical understanding
- **Cognition** was the lowest-scoring domain — significant gaps in understanding how AI systems work and when to trust or override them

> [!important]
> The Cognition–Ethics inversion is the critical finding: students can identify that AI raises ethical concerns but cannot evaluate *when* an AI output is trustworthy. This is exactly the knowledge base required to avoid [[Automation Bias]] in clinical practice.

---

## Why the Cognition Gap Is Dangerous

Low Cognition scores combined with AI deployment → the conditions for automation bias:
- Clinician recognises AI is fallible in principle (Ethics domain) but cannot identify *specific* failure modes
- In the time-pressured clinical moment, defaults to accepting AI output ([[Skills Rules and Knowledge]] → skill-based behaviour overrides deliberate evaluation)
- Pattern: "I know AI can be wrong, but I don't know when, so I follow it anyway"

This mirrors the [[Automation Bias Systematic Review — Goddard et al. 2012]] finding that erroneous CDSS advice is followed significantly more often than correct manual decisions.

---

## Structural Misalignment (Edwards et al. 2026 finding)

The survey found:
- Clinical AI exposure (using AI tools in placements) was the strongest predictor of preparedness across all four domains
- Formal education about AI was patchy and inconsistently delivered
- Students who had neither clinical exposure nor formal education were substantially less prepared

**Implication**: curriculum reform is insufficient if clinical placements don't include real AI tools; and clinical exposure is insufficient without conceptual scaffolding (Cognition domain).
