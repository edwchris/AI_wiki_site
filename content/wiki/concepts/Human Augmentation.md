# Human Augmentation

The use of AI or technological tools to extend, enhance, or complement human cognitive and physical capabilities — as distinct from AI *replacing* human functions. The augmentation framing positions AI as a tool that makes humans more capable rather than one that substitutes for human judgment.

---

## Core Idea

Augmentation research asks: not "can AI do this task?" but "can AI make humans better at this task?" The distinction matters because:

- Human-AI teams can sometimes outperform both humans alone and AI alone
- The conditions under which augmentation works are different from conditions under which replacement works
- Augmentation preserves human skill and accountability while gaining AI efficiency

---

## Evidence Base

[[When Combinations of Humans and AI Are Useful — Vaccaro et al. 2024]] (meta-analysis, n=106 studies) provides the most rigorous available evidence:

- Human-AI teams outperform AI alone in **49% of tasks** and humans alone in **82% of tasks**
- But this aggregate masks enormous heterogeneity: the benefit depends on task type, AI accuracy, and human expertise
- The team advantage is largest when humans and AI have **complementary error profiles** — they fail in different ways

---

## When Augmentation Works

| Condition | Mechanism |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Complementary errors**: human and AI fail on different cases | Human detects AI failures; AI fills human blind spots |
| **Interpretable AI output**: human can assess AI reasoning | Calibrated verification; targeted acceptance or rejection |
| **Appropriate task coupling**: AI assists without taking over | Human remains engaged and skilled |
| **Calibrated trust**: human neither blindly accepts nor reflexively rejects AI | Optimal use of AI advantage |

---

## When Augmentation Fails

- AI accuracy is very high → human is redundant, introduces noise
- Task requires holistic judgment that AI atomises → AI output misleads rather than supplements
- Human cannot assess AI confidence → [[Automation Bias]] fills the gap
- Augmentation in training prevents skill development → [[Deliberate Practice]] argument
