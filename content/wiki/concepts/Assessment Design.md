# Assessment Design

The principled design of assessment tasks, methods, and conditions to produce valid judgments of student learning. In the AI era, assessment design is being forced to adapt because AI can complete most conventional unsupervised take-home tasks.

---

## The Core Challenge (Dawson 2025)

AI can now produce passing-level work on most conventional assessment tasks with good prompting. This creates a validity crisis: assessment tasks designed to evaluate student competency may instead evaluate prompting skill or AI tool access.

Two inadequate responses:
- **Prohibition**: instructing students not to use AI (unenforceable; hurts validity via surveillance)
- **"AI-proof" design**: creating tasks AI "can't do" — not a viable long-term strategy given AI's trajectory

The principled response is **structural change** — redesigning what is assessed, not just adding instructions about AI use.

---

## Structural vs. Discursive Change

| Type | Description | Limitation |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **Discursive** | Address AI through instructions (AI-scale designations, use restrictions) | Unenforceable; cannot trust compliance |
| **Structural** | Address AI through unavoidable design (supervised tasks, oral vivas, process check-ins) | High confidence; workload-intensive |

Dawson's position: accept that all unsupervised tasks will see significant AI use. Invest in securing ~6 high-confidence assessments per degree program. See [[Assessment Design for a Time of AI — Dawson 2025]].

---

## Key Principles (TEXA Guidance)

1. **Assessment for learning**: equip students for a world where AI is ubiquitous — including appropriate, ethical AI use
2. **Assessment of learning**: form trustworthy judgments via multiple, inclusive, contextualized approaches

Both must be held simultaneously — neither alone is sufficient.

---

## Design Tools

- **Future authentic assessment**: what does practice look like in the discipline *with* AI integration?
- **Cognitive offloading analysis**: distinguish intrinsic load (cannot be offloaded) from extraneous load (can)
- **Reverse scaffolding**: students demonstrate competency independently before using AI tools
- **Zone of proximal development**: AI production tools do not respect the ZPD; pedagogical AI tools may
- **Validity framing**: "does student AI use impede or support our ability to judge if they've met the outcome?"
