# AI Education in Medical Imaging

Preparation of students and practitioners in medical imaging professions (sonography, radiography, radiation therapy) for working alongside AI tools — encompassing AI literacy, critical appraisal, and professional accountability.

---

## The Preparedness Gap

Evidence from Australian MRS education (Edwards et al. 2026 — [[Measuring AI Preparedness in Health Professions Education — Edwards et al. 2026]]):
- Using **MAIRS-MS** framework (Cognition, Ability, Vision, Ethics):
  - **Ethics domain**: highest scores — students feel ethically oriented to AI
  - **Cognition domain**: lowest scores — weakest foundational AI knowledge
- This imbalance is dangerous: students may believe they should challenge AI outputs, but lack the technical knowledge to know *when or how*

> [!important]
> Ethical orientation without AI knowledge may paradoxically *reduce* safety: practitioners may over-defer to AI or under-defer with poor rationale if they lack technical understanding.

---

## Factors That Improve Preparedness

From Edwards et al. 2026:
| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Formal AI education in curriculum | +8.6 points (MAIRS-MS, p = 0.01) |
| Clinical exposure to AI tools | +10.6 points (MAIRS-MS, p < 0.01) |

Both are significant and additive — classroom AI coverage alone is insufficient.

## Curriculum Design Principles

Derived from cross-wiki evidence:

| Principle | Source |
|-----------|--------|
| Strengthen foundational AI knowledge (Cognition) | [[Measuring AI Preparedness in Health Professions Education — Edwards et al. 2026]] |
| Integrate authentic clinical AI encounters (not just classroom AI) | [[Measuring AI Preparedness in Health Professions Education — Edwards et al. 2026]] |
| Teach professional accountability explicitly — what to do when AI is wrong | [[The Role of Patient Outcomes in Moral Responsibility — Edwards et al. 2025]] |
| Distinguish clinical AI (diagnostic tools) from generative AI (LLMs) — different competencies required | [[Measuring AI Preparedness in Health Professions Education — Edwards et al. 2026]] |
| Preserve deliberate practice opportunities — AI automation risks reducing hands-on skill-building | [[Deliberate Practice in Sonography — Edwards et al. 2018]], [[Deliberate Practice]] |
| Prepare for automation bias and calibration failure, not just technical operation | [[AI vs. Clinicians — Nagendran et al. 2020]], [[Human-Centered Evaluation of AI in Diabetic Retinopathy Clinics — Beede et al. 2020]] |

## Structural Misalignment Problem

A key finding from Edwards et al. 2026: university AI teaching lags behind what students encounter on clinical placement. Students are exposed to AI tools in the clinic that were never covered in formal education.

This creates:
1. Unprepared students making real clinical decisions with AI tools
2. Inconsistent baseline: students at AI-rich sites develop competency; others do not
3. Graduates with variable and unaudited AI capabilities entering the workforce

---

## Moral Responsibility in AI-Assisted Practice

Practitioners do not perceive moral responsibility as fixed — it shifts with outcome framing (Edwards et al. 2025 — [[The Role of Patient Outcomes in Moral Responsibility — Edwards et al. 2025]]):
- Positive outcomes → practitioner claims greater individual responsibility
- Adverse outcomes → responsibility disperses toward AI developer and institution

Curriculum implication: teach students to maintain consistent accountability regardless of outcome — *prospective* accountability framing, not *retrospective*.

---

## Connection to Deskilling Risk

AI automation that removes procedural tasks (e.g., PROMETHEUS — AI detects planes, sonographer reviews) reduces opportunities for [[Deliberate Practice]]:
- Expert skill requires structured, effortful practice with feedback
- Routine task automation may produce time savings at the cost of building the expert skill base
- Long-term workforce implication: graduates may depend on AI without having developed independent imaging expertise

See: [[Deliberate Practice in Sonography — Edwards et al. 2018]], [[PROMETHEUS RCT — Day et al. 2025]]
