**Citation:** Edwards, C., Murphy, A., Singh, A., Daniel, S., & Chamunyonga, C. (2025). The role of patient outcomes in shaping moral responsibility in AI-supported decision making. *Radiography*, 31(3), 102948. DOI: 10.1016/j.radi.2025.102948

**Raw file:** not directly identified (see log note)

## Summary
Investigates how medical radiation practitioners perceive and attribute moral responsibility when AI decision-support tools contribute to patient outcomes — positive and negative. Reveals that outcome framing significantly shapes accountability attribution.

## Methodology
- Cross-sectional online survey, September–December 2024
- 649 international medical radiation practitioners
- Design: random assignment to one of four profession-specific scenarios pairing AI recommendation with different patient outcomes (positive/negative)
- Measures: 5-point Likert scale for moral responsibility attribution; Kruskal-Wallis tests; ordinal regression

## Key Findings
- **Patient outcomes significantly influence moral responsibility perceptions** (p < 0.001)
- Practitioners assigned **higher moral responsibility to themselves** in positive outcomes
- In **adverse outcomes**, responsibility shifted toward shared accountability (practitioner + AI developer + institution)
- Approximately one-third attributed shared responsibility to AI developers and/or institutions
- **Prior knowledge of AI ethics** significantly shaped responsibility perceptions

## Implications
1. Moral responsibility for AI-assisted decisions is not stable — it shifts with outcome framing (outcome bias / hindsight bias in ethics)
2. Institutions and AI developers are increasingly seen as accountable co-parties when AI contributes to adverse outcomes
3. AI ethics education must prepare practitioners for this complexity — not just "who is responsible" but "how does outcome affect that perception"
4. Shared accountability models are emerging organically in practitioner cognition; curriculum and governance frameworks should formalize this
