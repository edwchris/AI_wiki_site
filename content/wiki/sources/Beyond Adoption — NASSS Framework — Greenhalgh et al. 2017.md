# Beyond Adoption — NASSS Framework — Greenhalgh et al. 2017

**Full citation:** Greenhalgh T, Wherton J, Papoutsi C, et al. Beyond adoption: A new framework for theorizing and evaluating nonadoption, abandonment, and challenges to the scale-up, spread, and sustainability of health and care technologies. *J Med Internet Res*. 2017;19(11):e367.

**DOI:** 10.2196/jmir.8775

---

## What it is

A theory-informed, evidence-based framework for predicting and evaluating why technology-supported health and care programs fail to achieve adoption, scale-up, spread, or long-term sustainability. Derived from a hermeneutic systematic review of 28 prior implementation frameworks plus 6 longitudinal empirical case studies conducted across 20+ UK healthcare organisations (2011–2017).

## The NASSS Framework

![[12916_2019_1463_Fig1_HTML.png]]

Seven domains, each classified as **simple** (predictable), **complicated** (multiple interacting components), or **complex** (dynamic, unpredictable):

| Domain | Key question |
|--------|-------------|
| 1. The condition | How characterised, predictable, and sociocultural in the target population? |
| 2. The technology | Material features, knowledge type generated, knowledge to use it, supply model |
| 3. Value proposition | Upstream (investor/business) vs downstream (patient/payer) value |
| 4. Adopter system | Staff (identity, practice), patient (burden, capability), lay caregivers (network) |
| 5. Organisation(s) | Capacity to innovate, readiness, adoption decision, team routines, implementation work |
| 6. Wider context | Political, economic, regulatory, professional, sociocultural environment |
| 7. Embedding over time | Scope for adaptation; organisational resilience and reflexive monitoring |

> [!important] Core finding: Programs with **complexity in multiple domains** rarely achieve mainstreaming. Complicatedness can be managed; complexity across many domains cannot be overcome.

## Empirical basis

Six case studies across UK health and social care settings:
- Video outpatient consultations (Skype/FaceTime)
- GPS tracking for cognitive impairment
- Pendant alarms
- Remote biomarker monitoring in heart failure
- Care-organizing software
- Integrated case management via data warehouse

Data: >400 hours ethnographic observation, 165 semi-structured interviews, 200 documents.

## Key theoretical contributions

- Shifts frame from "barriers and facilitators" to dynamic interactions between domains
- Distinguishes simple/complicated/complex phenomena (complexity theory)
- Highlights that implementation work is extensive, hidden, and underestimated
- Professional staff acceptance = single most important factor at local level
- Local champions essential for sense-making and embedding
- Distinguishes "cold" (biomarker) vs "warm" (communication) health technologies
