**Citation:** Middleton, J. M., & Bolderston, A. (2023). Engagement in radiation therapy research: What happens after graduation? *Journal of Medical Imaging and Radiation Sciences*, 54, 328–334. DOI: 10.1016/j.jmir.2023.03.026

**Raw file:** `1-s2.0-S1939865423000504 (1).pdf`

**Type:** Survey study

## Summary
Examines the long-term impact of undergraduate research education on radiation therapy (RT) graduates' research engagement. Surveyed alumni of the University of Alberta RT Program who graduated 2017–2020. Core question: does learning to do research as an undergraduate translate into doing research as a clinician?

## Methods
- Survey of RT graduates, University of Alberta, 2017–2020
- Examined: dissemination of undergraduate research projects; whether projects led to changes in practice, policy, or patient care; whether graduates conducted subsequent research; motivators and barriers to post-graduation research

## Key Findings
- Research engagement post-graduation is low relative to the effort invested in undergraduate research education
- Dissemination of student projects (conference presentation, publication) is limited
- Common barriers include: time, lack of mentorship, heavy clinical workload, unclear institutional support
- Motivators include: patient care improvement, professional development, supervisor/colleague encouragement

## Significance
Identifies the gap between research education and research practice — the same structural mismatch that [[Radiographer Research Engagement — Stewart-Lord et al. 2026]] addresses internationally. Relevant to understanding why AI implementation research in radiography may lag clinical adoption: the workforce is not equipped or supported to generate it.
