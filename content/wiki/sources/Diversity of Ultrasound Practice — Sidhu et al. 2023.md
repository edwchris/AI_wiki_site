**Citation:** Sidhu, P.S. et al. (2023). Diversity of current ultrasound practice within and outside radiology departments with a vision for 20 years into the future: a position paper of the ESR ultrasound subcommittee. *Insights into Imaging*, 14(1), 202. DOI: 10.1186/s13244-023-01548-w

**Raw file:** `s13244-023-01548-w.pdf`

## Summary
Position paper from the European Society of Radiology (ESR) documenting heterogeneity of ultrasound practice across Europe and globally, with a 20-year forward projection for the field.

## Key Themes

### Practice Heterogeneity
- Ultrasound is performed by multiple clinician groups: radiologists, cardiologists, obstetricians, emergency physicians, point-of-care users
- Training standards, accreditation requirements, and quality assurance vary widely across countries and specialties
- Lack of standardization creates quality gaps and ambiguous accountability

### 20-Year Vision
- AI-assisted image acquisition expected to lower technical barriers, enabling broader point-of-care ultrasound use
- Potential shift: AI handles acquisition mechanics; specialist interprets
- Workforce planning must account for AI-driven workflow changes: different skill sets will be valued
- Risk: quality heterogeneity may worsen as less-trained users rely on AI that may not generalize to their patient population

## Relevance to AI
- Heterogeneous practice creates heterogeneous training data → exacerbates [[AI Ground Truth]] problems
- AI standardization could reduce quality variation but requires robust validation across diverse practice contexts
- Who is responsible for AI-assisted ultrasound performed outside radiology departments? Accountability is unclear
- Connects to [[Developing and Implementing AI Tools in Radiology — Multisociety 2023]] governance framework

> [!note] Scope
