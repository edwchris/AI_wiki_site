## Summary
Brief report establishing the learning curve — the minimum number of cases required to achieve analyst competency — for left atrial strain (LAS) analysis using speckle tracking echocardiography. LAS is a technically demanding measurement with known operator and software dependence; determining when analysts reach competency is critical for clinical implementation.

## Context: Why LAS Learning Curves Matter
- Left atrial strain (particularly reservoir strain, LASr) is an emerging biomarker for atrial fibrillation risk, diastolic dysfunction, and heart failure
- Unlike ejection fraction, LAS requires semi-automated tracing with manual editing of regions of interest — operator-dependent
- No standardised training curriculum exists; this study addresses the competency gap
- Connects directly to [[Deliberate Practice]] and [[Skills Rules and Knowledge]] frameworks

## Key Questions the Paper Addresses
- How many LAS analyses must an operator perform before reaching a stable, competent level?
- Is LAS a skill that can be acquired with a defined number of supervised cases?
