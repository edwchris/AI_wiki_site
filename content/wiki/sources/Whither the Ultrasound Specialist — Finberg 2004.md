**Citation:** Finberg, H. J. (2004). Whither (wither?) the ultrasound specialist? *Journal of Ultrasound in Medicine*, 23(12), 1543–1547. DOI: 10.7863/jum.2004.23.12.1543

**Raw file:** `Finberg-2004-Whither (Wither_) the Ultrasound.pdf`

## Summary
Opinion piece by a radiologist questioning the long-term viability of ultrasound as a specialist skill as point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) proliferates among non-radiologist clinicians (emergency physicians, intensivists, cardiologists). The title pun ("whither" = where are we going / "wither" = decay) frames the central concern: will specialist ultrasound expertise erode as the skill diffuses?

## Core Argument
- Specialised ultrasound expertise (sonographer/radiologist) developed because:
  1. Equipment was expensive and complex
  2. Training was intensive and time-consuming
  3. Interpretation required deep domain knowledge
- POCUS is changing all three conditions: cheaper, simpler equipment; briefer training; task-specific rather than comprehensive interpretation
- Risk: diffusion of partial competence across many practitioners → loss of deep expertise concentration in ultrasound specialists

## Relevance (2026 perspective)
- Written 20 years before AI-assisted ultrasound; the concern about skill erosion has returned in a new form
- AI-guided POCUS now enables even less-trained operators to obtain diagnostic-quality images → extends the POCUS diffusion trend Finberg identified
- The "wither" question is now doubly relevant: not just from POCUS proliferation but from AI automation of acquisition and interpretation
