**Citation:** Rajpurkar, P., Chen, E., Banerjee, O., & Topol, E. J. (2022). AI in health and medicine. *Nature Medicine*, 28, 31–38. DOI: 10.1038/s41591-021-01614-0

**Raw file:** `s41591-021-01614-0.pdf`

## Summary
High-level review of AI's trajectory in medicine, co-authored by [[Pranav Rajpurkar]] and [[Eric Topol]]. Surveys current applications across medical imaging, EHR analysis, drug discovery, and genomics; identifies persistent challenges and proposes a research agenda. Provides the broad landscape context for more specific studies in this wiki.

## Scope of Coverage
- **Medical imaging**: radiology, pathology, ophthalmology, dermatology
- **Electronic health records**: predictive modelling, clinical NLP
- **Genomics and drug discovery**: molecular property prediction, clinical trial optimisation
- **Clinical decision support**: sepsis prediction, mortality risk

## Key Claims

### What AI does well (2022 state)
- Image classification at or above specialist level in narrow, well-defined tasks (diabetic retinopathy, skin lesion, chest X-ray)
- Prognostic prediction from EHR data
- Speed and throughput in screening contexts

### Persistent Challenges
- **Generalisation**: models trained on data from one site/population underperform at new sites
- **Prospective validation**: most evidence is retrospective; randomised trials are rare
- **Clinical workflow integration**: accuracy benchmarks do not predict implementation success (see [[Human-Centered Evaluation of AI in Diabetic Retinopathy Clinics — Beede et al. 2020]])
- **Explainability**: current models provide limited rationale for clinicians
- **Equity**: AI trained on non-representative data may worsen health disparities

### Proposed priorities
1. More prospective and adaptive clinical trials
2. Multi-site validation studies
3. Standardised reporting requirements
4. Attention to implementation as a distinct research domain
