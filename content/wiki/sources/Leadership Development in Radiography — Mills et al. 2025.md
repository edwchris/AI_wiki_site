**Citation:** Mills, K., Snaith, B., & Williams, A. (2025). Opportunities for leadership development in radiography, a scoping review. *Radiography*, 31, 102935. DOI: 10.1016/j.radi.2025.102935

**Raw file:** `PIIS1078817425000768.pdf`

**Type:** Scoping review

## Summary
Scoping review identifying where leadership opportunities exist for the radiographic workforce and how these are supported, valued, and actioned. Search covered 20 years of literature; 25 documents included (23 studies, 2 policy documents), primarily from UK and Australia.

## Key Findings

**Core competencies identified:**
- Team collaboration and communication skills — most consistently highlighted
- Interprofessional skills
- Visionary thinking

**Challenges:**
- Resource constraints
- Role ambiguity — leadership is often added to clinical roles without structural support
- Lack of formal leadership training pathways in radiography curricula

**Recommendations:**
- Evidence-based leadership training embedded in radiography education
- Role alignment: clarity about what leadership means at different career stages
- Support for radiography managers navigating the clinical-to-management transition

## Significance
Leadership capacity in radiography is relevant to AI implementation: effective radiography leaders are needed to evaluate, procure, and oversee AI tools at departmental level. Without leadership infrastructure, AI deployment defaults to clinician improvisation — the pattern identified in [[Implementing AI Decision Support in Radiology — JMIR 2026]] and [[Computer Technology and Clinical Work — Wears and Berg 2005]].

The role ambiguity finding mirrors concerns in [[21st Century Sonographer — Thomas et al. 2017]] about professional identity and scope — leadership ambiguity is an extension of the same structural problem.
