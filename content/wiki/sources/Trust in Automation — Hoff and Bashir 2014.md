**Citation:** Hoff, K. A., & Bashir, M. (2014). Trust in automation: Integrating empirical evidence on factors that influence trust. *Human Factors*, 56(3), 407–434. DOI: 10.1177/0018720813492251

**Raw file:** `hoff-bashir-2014-trust-in-automation.pdf`

## Summary
Systematic integration of empirical evidence on trust in automation, extending Lee & See (2004). Proposes a three-layer model of trust: dispositional, situational, and learned. Provides the most comprehensive taxonomy of trust antecedents available at time of publication.

## Three-Layer Trust Model

| Layer | Description | Source |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| **Dispositional trust** | Stable individual difference — general tendency to trust; shaped by personality and culture | Trait-level |
| **Situational trust** | Context-specific trust shaped by task, environment, and immediate experience | State-level |
| **Learned trust** | Accumulated from experience with this specific automation system | Interaction-level |

> [!note]
> This layered model explains why identical automation systems can elicit different trust responses across individuals and contexts: dispositional differences modulate how situational factors are interpreted.

## Factors Influencing Trust (Empirical Summary)

### System factors
- **Reliability**: most consistent predictor of learned trust
- **Predictability**: consistent, understandable system behaviour builds trust
- **Transparency**: visible rationale increases trust (but may also reduce appropriate scepticism)
- **Failure modes**: dramatic failures (false alarms vs. misses) differentially affect trust decay

### Human factors
- **Experience**: more experienced operators develop better-calibrated trust
- **Cognitive style**: analytic thinkers develop more calibrated trust than intuitive thinkers
- **Workload**: under high workload, trust is used as a shortcut (increasing over-reliance)

### Task/context factors
- **Risk**: higher stakes increase scrutiny and reduce over-trust
- **Time pressure**: forces heuristic reliance on automation; increases complacency
- **Domain expertise**: experts are better calibrated but not immune to bias

## Comparison with Lee & See 2004

Lee & See [[Trust in Automation — Lee and See 2004]] focused on design principles; Hoff & Bashir add the three-layer taxonomy and a more detailed empirical synthesis of antecedents. Together they provide the standard framework for thinking about trust calibration in applied settings.
