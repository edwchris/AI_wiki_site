**Citation:** Strauch, B. (2017). Ironies of automation: Still unresolved after all these years. *IEEE Transactions on Human-Machine Systems*, 48(5), 419–433. DOI: 10.1109/THMS.2017.2732506

**Raw file:** `roniesofutomationtillnresolvedfterllheseears_4.pdf` (garbled download filename = "Ironies of automation still unresolved after all these years")

## Summary
Critical re-assessment of Bainbridge's 1983 "Ironies of Automation" 34 years later. Concludes that the core ironies remain largely unresolved despite decades of research, and identifies new ironies created by increasingly sophisticated automation. A key text for contextualising AI in the continuity of human-automation problems.

## Key Findings

### Core ironies still unresolved (34 years later)
- **Skill degradation**: manual proficiency still atrophies in automated environments; retraining costs remain high
- **Mode confusion**: operators still struggle to know what automated system is doing and why
- **Complacency / automation bias**: remains widespread despite training interventions
- **Takeover failure**: cognitive and skill gaps when resuming manual control in emergencies remain a persistent cause of incidents

### New ironies created by modern automation
- **Opacity**: contemporary automation (neural networks, complex algorithms) is more opaque than 1983 systems — operators can no longer reconstruct system logic even in principle
- **Automation of supervision**: automated monitoring of automation creates new failure modes (meta-automation)
- **Accountability diffusion**: distributed automation obscures who is responsible for errors

## Connection to AI in Healthcare
- Modern AI diagnostic tools instantiate all the classic Bainbridge ironies with amplified opacity: the DNN cannot explain its own decisions
- The "accountability diffusion" irony maps directly onto the findings of [[The Role of Patient Outcomes in Moral Responsibility — Edwards et al. 2025]] — responsibility is distributed but diffusely
- The "still unresolved" conclusion is a strong argument against the optimistic view that explanable AI (XAI) or better interfaces will resolve these problems
