**Citation:** Van Der Westhuizen, L., Naidoo, K., Casmod, Y., & Mdletshe, S. (2020). A Qualitative Approach to Understanding the Effects of a Caring Relationship Between the Sonographer and Patient. *Journal of Medical Imaging and Radiation Sciences*, 51(Suppl), S53–S58. DOI: 10.1016/j.jmir.2020.07.005

**Raw file:** `1-s2.0-S1939865420301612.pdf`

**Published as part of a conference supplement issue** (S53–S58 page range indicates supplement).

## Study Design
- Qualitative, exploratory, descriptive, contextual, phenomenological design
- 4 focus group interviews with 14 qualified sonographers in private practice, Gauteng, South Africa
- Data collection continued to saturation

## Key Findings

**Dual effects of the caring relationship:**
| Effect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Positive | Professional pride; sense of meaning and purpose; emotional reward |
| Negative | Emotional strain; compassion fatigue; burnout risk |

**Protective strategies:**
- Sonographers spontaneously developed psychological distancing and coping mechanisms
- These were informal and unsupported by workplace structures

**Practical guidelines produced:**
- Establish supportive reflective practice culture
- Access to psychological support services
- Physical wellness spaces in the workplace

## Significance
Complements the role-boundary concerns in [[21st Century Sonographer — Thomas et al. 2017]]:
- Thomas et al. focus on communication of adverse findings (what sonographers must not say)
- Van Der Westhuizen et al. focus on the emotional cost of deep patient engagement (what sonographers absorb)
- Together they frame the **hidden emotional labour** of sonography, rarely acknowledged in discussions of AI role change

The arrival of AI may alter this dimension unpredictably: if AI flags pathology before the sonographer sees it, who bears the emotional burden of the discovery?

## Limitations
- South African private practice context — may not generalise to Australian or NHS public hospital settings
- Small sample (n=14); supplement publication suggests conference-adjacent rather than flagship venue
