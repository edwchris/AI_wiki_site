**Citation:** Hollan, J., Hutchins, E., & Kirsh, D. (2000). Distributed cognition: Toward a new foundation for human-computer interaction research. *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction*, 7(2), 174–196. DOI: 10.1145/353485.353487

**Raw file:** `Hollan Distributed cognition.pdf`

## Summary
Foundational position paper proposing distributed cognition (DC) as the theoretical basis for HCI research. Argues that cognition spans individuals, artifacts, and environments — and that HCI must study the entire sociotechnical system, not just the interface. Directly applicable to evaluating AI tools as components of clinical cognitive systems.

## Core Principles of Distributed Cognition

### 1. Cognitive processes may be distributed across members of a group
- Teams share cognitive load through specialisation, parallel processing, coordination
- No single person holds the full "cognitive map" of a complex system

### 2. Cognitive processes involve coordination with material and environmental structure
- Artefacts (instruments, displays, checklists) are cognitive media — they store, transform, and transmit representational states
- AI is a cognitive artefact that participates in distributed clinical cognition

### 3. Processes may be distributed across time
- Past cognitive work (prior reports, protocol development) shapes present performance through media that persist

## What DC Analysis Reveals
A DC analysis of a clinical AI system would ask:
- How does information enter the AI? (What cognitive work precedes it?)
- What does the AI do with it? (What transformation occurs?)
- How does AI output propagate to clinical action? (What cognitive steps follow?)
- Where are the breakdown points? (Where do transformations fail or lose fidelity?)

## Contrast with Individual Cognition Approach
| Approach | Unit of analysis | What it misses |
|----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Traditional cognitive psychology | Individual mind | Artefacts, coordination, context |
| Distributed cognition | Functional system | Individual variability |

DC does not deny individual cognition — it extends the unit of analysis to capture what individual-centred approaches miss.

## Relevance to AI Evaluation
- [[Human-Centered Evaluation of AI in Diabetic Retinopathy Clinics — Beede et al. 2020]] implicitly uses a DC lens: the AI's failure was a failure of the AI-in-the-sociotechnical-system, not just an AI technical failure
- Connects to [[Human-Centered AI Evaluation]] — DC analysis is a method for identifying where AI creates new vulnerabilities in the clinical cognitive system
