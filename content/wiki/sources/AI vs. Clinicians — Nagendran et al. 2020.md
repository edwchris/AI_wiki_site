**Citation:** Nagendran, M., Chen, Y., Lovejoy, C.A., Gordon, A.C., Komorowski, M., Harvey, H., Topol, E.J., Ioannidis, J.P.A., Collins, G.S. & Maruthappu, M. (2020). Artificial intelligence versus clinicians: systematic review of design, reporting standards, and claims of deep learning studies in medical imaging. *BMJ*, 368, m689.

**Raw file:** `Nagendran et al. - 2020 - Artificial intellige.pdf`

## Summary
Systematic review of trials comparing [[Deep Learning in Medical Imaging]] performance against clinicians; examines methodology and validity of published claims.

## Key Findings
- 81 published non-randomized trials identified; only 10 registered trials
- 61/81 studies (75%) claimed AI was comparable or superior to clinicians
- **But:** most studies were non-prospective, high risk of bias, deviated from reporting standards
- Few studies conducted in real-world clinical settings; most used retrospective held-out test sets
- Prospective RCTs of AI in clinical imaging were essentially absent at time of publication

## Methodology
- Searched for randomized and non-randomized trials comparing DL algorithms vs. clinicians in diagnostic imaging
- Applied TRIPOD, CONSORT, and SPIRIT reporting guidelines to assess study quality

## Implications
- The published AI literature is overwhelmingly optimistic, but rigorous evaluation is rare
- Performance on benchmark datasets does not equal real-world clinical utility → [[AI Ground Truth]]
- Subsequent work (e.g., [[PROMETHEUS RCT — Day et al. 2025]]) addresses this methodological gap by using prospective RCT design
