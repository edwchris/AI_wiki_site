## Summary
Joint statement from seven North American and European radiology and medical imaging societies; establishes ethical principles for AI development, deployment, and governance in radiology.

## Core Ethical Principles
1. **Beneficence** — AI use must benefit patients and the common good; use that generates financial gain without patient benefit should be blocked
2. **Non-maleficence** — AI errors cause harm; accountability must be clear before deployment
3. **Fairness and bias** — AI can encode and amplify demographic, comorbidity, and technical biases
4. **Transparency** — known failure modes must be disclosed; interpretability is desirable but contested
5. **Autonomy** — patients must have meaningful consent over AI use of their data and images
6. **Justice** — AI should not widen health inequities across populations

## Key Issues Identified
- Bias sources: demographic underrepresentation, data shift, covariate shift, comorbidity confounding
- Accountability gaps: radiologist, vendor, or institution? Current frameworks do not clearly assign liability
- Transparency vs. IP: vendors resist training data and architecture disclosure; regulators and clinicians need it
- Adaptive AI: FDA/CE frameworks designed for static software; adaptive AI that learns post-deployment is unresolved

## Practical Recommendations
- Radiology community should develop codes of practice that promote patient-benefiting AI and block extractive use
- Algorithms should be tested on diverse, representative populations before deployment
- Post-market surveillance required for performance monitoring
