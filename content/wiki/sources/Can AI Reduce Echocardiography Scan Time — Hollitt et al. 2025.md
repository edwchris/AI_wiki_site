**Citation:** Hollitt, K. J., Milanese, S., Joseph, M. et al. (2025). Can automation and artificial intelligence reduce echocardiography scan time and ultrasound system interaction? *Echo Research & Practice*, 12, 77. DOI: 10.1186/s44156-025-00077-0

**Raw file:** `s44156-025-00077-0.pdf`

## Summary
Clinical study testing whether AI-assisted automation reduces scan time and system interaction burden during echocardiography. Directly extends the PROMETHEUS findings (fetal anomaly scan) to cardiac ultrasound, examining both efficiency and user experience outcomes.

## Key Findings
- AI-assisted scanning reduced time spent on system interaction (menu navigation, measurement input) compared to conventional acquisition
- Sonographer cognitive load was qualitatively reduced — AI guidance on probe positioning and automatic measurement capture freed attention for clinical assessment
- No reduction in image quality detected

## Significance
- Alongside [[PROMETHEUS RCT — Day et al. 2025]] and [[AI-Enhanced Cardiac US Training — Karni et al. 2025]], contributes to emerging evidence that AI-guided cardiac ultrasound delivers efficiency benefits
- Cardiac ultrasound is a particularly strong use case because echocardiography views are highly standardised — AI can reliably recognise target views and confirm acquisition quality
- Key question (not fully resolved here): does AI-assisted efficiency come at a cost to skill development for novices? (See [[Ironies of Automation]], [[Deliberate Practice]])
