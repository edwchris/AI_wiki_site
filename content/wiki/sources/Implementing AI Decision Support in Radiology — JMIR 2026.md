## Summary
Prospective qualitative study applying the NASSS framework to evaluate the implementation of an AI decision support system in a public hospital radiology department. Reveals how sociotechnical factors beyond pure technical performance determine whether AI tools are adopted and sustained in practice.

## The NASSS Framework
NASSS (Nonadoption, Abandonment, Scale-up, Spread, Sustainability) analyses technology implementation across seven domains:
1. **Condition** (patient/clinical complexity)
2. **Technology** (nature of the AI tool)
3. **Value proposition** (who benefits and how)
4. **Adopters** (clinicians, patients, others)
5. **Organisation** (structure, culture, readiness)
6. **Wider system** (regulatory, funding context)
7. **Embedding and adaptation** (over time)

## Key Findings

### Sociotechnical complexity
- AI tool performance (accuracy) was not the primary driver of adoption or abandonment
- Organisational readiness, workflow disruption, and trust dynamics were dominant factors
- Domains interact dynamically: a strength in one domain can be undermined by weakness in another

### Practical guidance for implementation
- Move from pilot to routine requires active co-design with end users — not just procurement and installation
- Time delay from image to report remains the key KPI driving AI adoption in public hospitals
- Trust in AI is built incrementally; initial deployment in lower-stakes contexts (prioritisation, not diagnosis) eases adoption
