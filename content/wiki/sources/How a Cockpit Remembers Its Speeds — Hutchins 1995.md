**Citation:** Hutchins, E. (1995). How a cockpit remembers its speeds. *Cognitive Science*, 19(3), 265–288. DOI: 10.1207/s15516709cog1903_1

**Raw file:** `Cognitive Science - July 1995 - Hutchins - How.pdf`

## Summary
Case study demonstrating that cognition is not confined to individual minds — it is distributed across people, tools, and artifacts. Analyses the speed-card system in a commercial aircraft cockpit as a cognitive system: memory and computation are embedded in the sociotechnical system, not just in the pilot. Foundational for distributed cognition theory.

## Core Argument
The cockpit "remembers" approach speeds not through any individual pilot's memory, but through:
1. The physical arrangement of speed cards and speed bugs
2. The procedural coordination between crew members
3. The progressive propagation of information through instruments and checklists

**Conclusion**: what appears to be individual memory is actually a property of the human-artifact system as a whole.

## Key Concepts

### Propagation of representational state
- Information is "computed" by transforming representations across media (verbal → instrument → physical → display)
- Each transformation is a step in a distributed cognitive process
- Errors arise at transformation boundaries — where information moves between representations

### Functional system
- The unit of analysis for cognition is the **functional system** (human + artifacts + environment), not the individual
- This challenges cognitive psychology's individual-centred model

## Implications for AI in Medical Imaging
- Diagnostic cognition in imaging is already distributed: radiologist + PACS system + prior reports + reference images
- AI inserts a new node into this distributed system
- Questions to ask: Where does the AI receive information? Where does it output? How does its output propagate through the rest of the cognitive system?
- AI failures at "transformation boundaries" (where AI output becomes clinical action) are a key concern — see [[Human-Centered Evaluation of AI in Diabetic Retinopathy Clinics — Beede et al. 2020]]
