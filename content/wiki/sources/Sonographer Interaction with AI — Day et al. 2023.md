**Citation:** Day, T. G., & Papageorghiou, A. T. (2023). Sonographer interaction with artificial intelligence: collaboration or conflict? *Ultrasound in Obstetrics & Gynecology*, 62(1), 9–15. DOI: 10.1002/uog.26238

**Raw file:** `Day et al. - 2023 - Sonographer interaction wi.pdf`

## Summary
Review and conceptual framing paper by the PROMETHEUS team lead (Thomas Day) examining how sonographers and AI tools interact during live scanning. Precursor to the PROMETHEUS RCT. Explores whether the relationship is collaborative or generates conflict, and proposes a framework for thinking about automation levels in fetal ultrasound.

## Key Arguments

### AI will occupy a spectrum
- Real-time ultrasound involves simultaneous acquisition and analysis — full automation is unlikely short/medium term
- AI deployment in fetal scanning will fall on a spectrum from human-dominant to machine-dominant
- The operative question is: which parts of the task should be automated, and at what level?

### Current evidence on AI-sonographer interaction
- **Plane detection tools**: reduce inter-operator variability; provide real-time confirmation that standard views are achieved; early studies show time savings
- **Biometry automation**: automated measurements (HC, BPD, FL) are clinically accurate and faster than manual measurement
- **Traffic-light feedback**: AI feedback during live scanning (green = good view, flag = look again) is acceptable to sonographers and improves consistency

### Collaboration or conflict?
- Collaboration when AI handles well-defined, measurable subtasks (plane confirmation, biometry) → frees sonographer attention for diagnostic interpretation
- Conflict risk when AI recommendations contradict sonographer judgment without transparent rationale → trust breakdown; workflow disruption

## Implications for Practice
- Role separation (AI handles acquisition quality and measurement; sonographer handles diagnosis and patient management) is the emerging practical model
- This anticipates the [[AI-Human Role Separation]] framework from [[Role Separation in AI-Human Radiology — Rajpurkar & Topol 2025]]
- PROMETHEUS RCT subsequently confirms the benefits of this model in a prospective trial → [[PROMETHEUS RCT — Day et al. 2025]]
