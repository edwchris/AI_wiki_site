**Citation:** Buch, V. H., Ahmed, I., & Maruthappu, M. (2018). Artificial intelligence in medicine: Current trends and future possibilities. *British Journal of General Practice*, 68(668), 143–144. DOI: 10.3399/bjgp18X695213

**Raw file:** `Buch, Ahmed, Maruthappu - 2018 - Artificial in.pdf`

## Summary
Brief perspective piece in BJGP surveying AI applications in medicine and arguing for primary care's engagement with the trend. Situates AI within the broader technological transformation of healthcare. Useful as a time-stamped overview of 2018 expectations — many of which have since been partially validated and partially revised.

## Key Points (2018 perspective)
- AI attracted more healthcare investment than any other sector in 2016
- Machine learning applications in diagnosis: skin lesion classification (Esteva et al. 2017), diabetic retinopathy (Gulshan et al. 2016), ECG interpretation
- Neural networks now exceed specialist performance in specific narrow tasks
- Primary care implications: AI may improve triage, monitoring of chronic disease, and diagnostic support in resource-limited settings

## Historical Value
This paper is now useful as a benchmark for 2018 expectations:
- Claims about near-term deployment have proven optimistic (real-world implementation is harder than benchmarks suggest)
- The "AI exceeds specialists" claim requires major qualification (see [[AI vs. Clinicians — Nagendran et al. 2020]])
- The promise of equity improvement has also proven complex ([[Human-Centered Evaluation of AI in Diabetic Retinopathy Clinics — Beede et al. 2020]])
