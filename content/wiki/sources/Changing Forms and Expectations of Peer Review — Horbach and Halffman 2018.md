**Citation:** Horbach, S. P. J. M., & Halffman, W. W. (2018). The changing forms and expectations of peer review. *Research Integrity and Peer Review*, 3, 8. DOI: 10.1186/s41073-018-0051-5

**Raw file:** `s41073-018-0051-5.pdf`

**Type:** Narrative review with typological framework

## Core Argument
Peer review is expected to do more than it was designed for. It has evolved from a lightweight quality filter into a system expected to assure accuracy, establish research hierarchies, ensure fairness, and prevent fraud — four demands that are in tension with each other and which the system was not built to meet.

## Historical Development
- Peer review as a formal system emerged primarily **after World War II**, not from the 17th century as commonly assumed
- The term "peer review" itself only appeared in the scientific literature in the **1960s**
- External reviewer systems became standard practice only in recent decades
- The expectation that peer review should *safeguard research integrity* is a **recent addition** — and a contested one

## The Four Expectations Now Placed on Peer Review

| Expectation | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Quality assurance** | Filtering inaccurate or methodologically weak work |
| **Hierarchy** | Establishing which research is most impactful/relevant |
| **Fairness** | Equal treatment regardless of institution, gender, nationality |
| **Integrity** | Detecting and preventing fraud, fabrication, and plagiarism |

> [!important]
> These four expectations are **incompatible** in practice: a system that favours high-impact, novel research (hierarchy) systematically disadvantages replication and incremental work (quality/accuracy); a system designed to catch fraud requires resources and expertise beyond standard peer review.

## Taxonomy of Peer Review Forms
The paper systematises peer review variants across four dimensions:

| Dimension | Key variants |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Selection conditions** | Timing (pre/post-publication); selectiveness |
| **Identities and access** | Single-blind, double-blind, open (named reviewers), open (published reports) |
| **Specialisation** | Statistical review; structured reporting checklists |
| **Technology integration** | Plagiarism detection; image verification; reader commentary (PubPeer model) |

### Notable forms:
- **Registered reports**: pre-data review; removes publication bias for positive results
- **PLoS ONE model**: review for methodological rigour only, not novelty
- **Post-publication review**: community assessment after publication (arXiv, PubPeer)
- **Cascading peer review**: rejected manuscripts forwarded to lower-tier journals with reviews

## The Epistemological Shift
Technology is shifting validation from **inter-subjective checking** by human experts toward **mechanical forms of objectivity** (automated statistics scanners, image verification tools). This mirrors the broader AI theme: replacing tacit expert judgment with codified algorithmic checks — with the same risks of false confidence and missed context.

## The Library-to-Database Transition
The scientific literature is transitioning from a library ("competing claims coexist") to a database ("accurate facts that can be corrected or removed"). Peer review was designed for the library model; the database model demands much more from it — specifically, the ability to retract and correct the record prospectively.

## Significance for This Wiki
This paper is not directly about AI in imaging but is relevant to:
1. **AI as a peer review tool**: the shift to automated statistical checking raises the same questions as AI in clinical imaging — can algorithmic verification replace expert judgment?
2. **Research base for AI claims**: the AI in medical imaging literature (see [[AI vs. Clinicians — Nagendran et al. 2020]], [[DL vs Clinicians Systematic Review — Liu et al. 2019]]) has documented severe methodological problems that peer review failed to catch — this paper provides the structural explanation for why
3. **Registered reports as a solution**: the registered report format is a direct response to the reproducibility crisis; the AI/medical imaging field would benefit from adopting it
