**Citation:** Edwards, C., Chamunyonga, C., Searle, B., & Reddan, T. (2022). The application of artificial intelligence in the sonography profession: Professional and educational considerations. *Ultrasound*, 30(4), 273–282. DOI: 10.1177/1742271X211072473

**Raw file:** `Edwards et al. - 2022 - The application of art.pdf`

## Summary
Overview of AI applications in diagnostic ultrasound and their implications for the sonography profession, with focus on how AI will reshape both clinical practice and education.

## AI Applications in Sonography (survey)
- **Standard plane detection**: AI identifies and saves target image planes (e.g., fetal biometry planes, cardiac views)
- **Automated biometric measurement**: head circumference, femur length, organ dimensions
- **Pathology detection**: thyroid nodule classification, breast lesion assessment, cardiac function quantification
- **Workflow optimization**: worklist prioritization, image quality scoring, report pre-population

## Professional Implications
- AI may shift the sonographer role from technical image acquisition toward analytical interpretation and patient-centered care
- Liability and accountability remain unresolved when AI contributes to a missed diagnosis
- Workforce disruption is possible but unlikely to be wholesale replacement — operator-dependent nature of ultrasound limits full automation

## Educational Considerations
- Sonography curricula need AI literacy: understanding AI capabilities, limitations, and failure modes
- Risk of **deskilling**: students who learn alongside AI may not develop the foundational [[Tacit and Explicit Knowledge]] that expert practice requires
- [[Deliberate Practice]] in image acquisition may be disrupted if AI automates the mechanical components before competency is established
- Need for simulation-based deliberate practice to complement AI-integrated clinical training

## Challenges
- AI models trained on narrow institutional datasets generalize poorly
- Sonographer image acquisition skill is not easily automated: probe pressure, patient positioning, real-time problem-solving
- Heterogeneity of ultrasound practice globally complicates standardization → [[Diversity of Ultrasound Practice — Sidhu et al. 2023]]
