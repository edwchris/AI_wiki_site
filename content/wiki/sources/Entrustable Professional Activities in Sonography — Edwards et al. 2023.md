## Summary

Industry survey (n=309 of 655 invited; 90% power) reporting stakeholder views on the ASAR entrustable professional activities (EPAs) for graduate general sonographers. Majority agreement for existing EPAs; divergent views on the structure of critical practice units; strong support for adding a Paediatric unit; broad consensus that on-call/emergency work should not be entrusted to new graduates.

---

## What is an EPA?

An EPA (entrustable professional activity) is a whole professional task entrusted to a practitioner without requiring direct supervision — in sonography, a complete ultrasound examination.

Critical distinction:
- **Competencies** are *acquired by* the individual (communication, probe manipulation, image interpretation)
- **EPAs** describe *work to be done* and the level of *trust* a supervisor places in the practitioner

A single EPA integrates multiple competencies simultaneously: performing a 1st-trimester dating scan requires communication + psychomotor skill + image interpretation + clinical reasoning + documentation.

> [!important]
> This integrative character of EPAs maps directly onto the SRK framework: routine EPAs are performed largely at **skill-based** and **rule-based** levels; novel or complex EPAs (and especially emergency work) demand **knowledge-based** reasoning. See [[Skills Rules and Knowledge]].

## ASAR EPA Structure

Six critical practice units for general sonography; graduates must demonstrate competency in 5/6 minimum:

| Critical Practice Unit | Core Graduate EPAs |
|-----------------------|--------------------|
| Abdominal | Abdomen, Female Pelvis, Male Pelvis, Urinary Tract, Major Vessels |
| Breast | Male and Female Breast |
| Musculoskeletal | Shoulder |
| Obstetrics & Gynaecology | Female Pelvis (TA & TV), 1st/2nd/3rd Trimester scans |
| Superficial Parts | Breast, Scrotum, Thyroid & anterior neck |
| Vascular (General) | DVT, Carotid |

Nine additional examinations received >70% agreement for inclusion as graduate EPAs; three relate to interventional imaging (aspiration, injection).

## Key Survey Findings

| Finding | N | % |
|---------|---|---|
| No change to EPA list | 171/309 | 55.3% |
| Recommend amendments | 138/309 | 44.7% |
| All existing EPAs retained with >80% agreement | — | 100% |
| Increase to 6/6 critical practice units | 141/309 | 45.6% |
| Maintain 5/6 model | 132/309 | 42.7% |
| Add Paediatric unit | 181/293 | 61.8% |
| **Emergency/on-call work NOT appropriate for new graduates** | — | **Broad consensus** |

> [!important]
> The consensus that new graduates are not ready for emergency/on-call work is an implicit **K-level policy**: emergency examinations are out-of-distribution, high-stakes cases requiring deliberate knowledge-based reasoning. This is precisely where AI assistance is least reliable. The profession has independently reached the conclusion that this level of performance requires post-graduation development — the same level that the calibration bootstrapping problem (see analysis) argues AI-from-day-one training may prevent from developing.

## Key Conceptual Arguments

### Competencies are transferable; EPAs are not
> "Individual competencies are transferable across domains of practice and entrustment is not simply related to experience in a specific examination type."

A sonographer competent in probe manipulation and clinical reasoning can apply these competencies to unfamiliar examinations — the S-level and K-level skills generalise. EPAs, however, are specific examinations requiring task-specific integration.

### Whole-examination performance ≠ sum of atomised skills
The paper critiques "atomisation" of competency assessment (OSCEs, checklists) — "there are dangers to the excessive quantification in evaluating human competence to a set of numbers." Overall holistic ratings reflect competence more faithfully than detailed checklists. This aligns with the Dreyfus expert intuition point: expert performance cannot be decomposed into rule-following.

### Simulation ≠ clinical reality
> "Simulation activities in healthcare still struggle to replicate clinical practice, and questions remain about the authenticity of acquired 'simulated competencies' and their clinical relevance."

This limitation applies directly to AI-guided simulation: a novice achieving a diagnostic image with real-time AI guidance has not demonstrated the same competency as one achieving it independently.

### Reflective practice bridges EPA and individual competency
The paper advocates reflective practice as the mechanism for transferring individual competencies into EPA-level work-readiness — aligning with [[Deliberate Practice]] and the Lifelong Learner unit in the [[Australian Sonographer Competency Framework — Edwards et al. 2022]].

## Stakeholder Implications

| Stakeholder | Key message |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Accrediting bodies (ASAR)** | Review the 5/6 model; consider Paediatric unit; address EPA overlap between Breast and Superficial Parts |
| **Course providers** | EPAs as capstone assessments, not just checklists; don't conflate simulated competency with work readiness; three days/week clinical minimum |
| **Employers/supervisors** | Active engagement with assessment; expert holistic judgment is valid; do not roster new graduates to on-call |
| **Students** | Competencies are theirs; EPAs reflect trust placed by supervisor; reflective practice bridges the gap |

## AI Implications (Not in Paper — Wiki Synthesis)

The EPA framework was developed without reference to AI assistance. Several implications follow:

1. **The current EPA standard does not distinguish** between "performed competently with AI guidance" and "performed competently independently" — a critical gap as AI tools become standard in clinical practice.
2. **AI scaffolding S+R level performance** may allow students to achieve EPA entrustment without the underlying psychomotor and rule-based fluency needed to sustain that performance when AI is unavailable or incorrect.
3. **Emergency/on-call exclusion** becomes more complex with AI: if AI improves performance in novel cases, does the same K-level threshold still hold? Or does AI-assisted emergency performance create false confidence?
4. **Interventional EPAs** (imaging for aspiration/injection) received 70% support for graduate inclusion — these may be the area where AI guidance most directly supports safe EPA expansion, because they require precise tool tracking that AI does well.
