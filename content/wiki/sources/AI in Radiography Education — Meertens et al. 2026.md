# AI in Radiography Education — Meertens et al. 2026

**Full citation:** Meertens R, Currie C, Mrija E, Ohene-Botwe B, Tucker R. Artificial intelligence in radiography education: Opportunity, challenge and professional responsibility. *Radiography*. 2026. doi:10.1016/j.radi.2026.x (PII: S1078-8174(26)00128-8)

**Source URL:** radiographyonline.com/article/S1078-8174(26)00128-8

---

## What it is

An editorial/opinion piece in the journal *Radiography* addressing the growing presence of generative AI tools (particularly large language models and image generators) in radiography higher education. Published 2026. Represents a consensus perspective from five co-authors across multiple UK and international radiography institutions.

## Core argument

Generative AI is already being used by radiography students (and staff) regardless of institutional policy. The profession must respond with proactive, informed guidance rather than reactive prohibition.

> [!important] The paper argues for a shift from punitive responses to AI use toward educational approaches — developing AI literacy as a professional competency rather than treating AI tools as a threat to academic integrity alone.

## Key themes

### AI Literacy as Professional Competency

- Radiographers working with AI in clinical settings need to critically evaluate AI outputs, not merely accept them
- AI literacy requires understanding capabilities AND limitations of AI tools
- Should be explicitly taught in undergraduate curricula, not assumed
- Cites [[AI in Medical Imaging Education — Crotty et al. 2024]] (ref 12 in paper: "Artificial intelligence in medical imaging education: recommendations for undergraduate curriculum development") — Christopher Edwards listed as co-author

### Academic Integrity Challenges

- Generative AI can produce convincing but incorrect clinical content (hallucinations)
- Students using GenAI for assignments may develop surface-level knowledge without deep clinical understanding
- Traditional assessment methods (essays, written reports) are highly vulnerable to AI generation
- Challenge: how to assess genuine clinical knowledge when AI can mimic the surface form

### Regulatory and Professional Context

- HCPC (UK Health and Care Professions Council) joint statement on AI in health professional education: February 2026
- Statement emphasises: ethical use, transparency, critical engagement
- Places responsibility on both education providers and students
- Aligns with UK Standards of Proficiency for Radiographers

### Recommendations

1. **Profession-led community of practice** — share pedagogical strategies across institutions
2. **Transparent, ethical, critically informed use** — not prohibition
3. **Revised assessment design** — move toward viva voce, practical simulation, in-person supervised tasks
4. **AI literacy curriculum integration** — systematic not ad hoc
5. **Student education on AI limitations** — including hallucination risks
