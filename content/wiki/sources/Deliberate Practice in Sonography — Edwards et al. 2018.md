**Citation:** Edwards, C., Chamunyonga, C., & Clarke, N. (2018). The role of deliberate practice in development of essential sonography skills. *Sonography*, 5(2), 76–81. DOI: 10.1002/sono.12146

**Raw file:** `Edwards, Chamunyonga, Clarke - 2018 - The role.pdf`

## Summary
Applies [[Deliberate Practice]] theory (Ericsson) to sonography skill development; argues that structured, goal-directed practice with feedback is necessary to develop expert-level ultrasound competency — routine clinical hours are insufficient.

## Key Arguments
- Sonography requires complex tacit skills: probe handling, real-time image interpretation, patient interaction, and clinical reasoning
- These skills develop through [[Deliberate Practice]], not mere exposure
- Curriculum design must go beyond clinical hour accumulation to incorporate structured DP principles
- Immediate, specific feedback on scanning performance is the critical accelerating factor

## Contrast: Routine vs. Deliberate Practice
| Routine Practice | Deliberate Practice |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Maintains skill level | Improves skill level |
| Low cognitive effort | High cognitive effort |
| Automated, habitual | Focused, effortful |
| Accumulates hours | Accumulates competency |

## Implications for AI
- AI tools that automate routine scanning tasks (e.g., plane detection, biometry → [[PROMETHEUS RCT — Day et al. 2025]]) may reduce the deliberate practice opportunities that build expert competency
- Students learning alongside AI risk developing dependency without acquiring foundational [[Tacit and Explicit Knowledge]]
- Pedagogical challenge: how to preserve deliberate practice opportunities as AI handles more of the mechanical work
